This is where your assistant actually learns. Upload the documents you want it to answer from, and it reads every page so it can find the right passage when a student asks.

## What you can upload

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="PDF" icon="file-text">
    Readings, articles, textbook chapters, problem sets.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Word" icon="file-type">
    `.docx` documents — notes, handouts, syllabi.
  </Card>
  <Card title="PowerPoint" icon="presentation">
    `.pptx` slide decks, including your speaker notes.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Text & Markdown" icon="file-code">
    Plain `.txt` and `.md` notes.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Spreadsheets & data" icon="table">
    `.csv` and `.json` files.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

:::note[File size and format notes]

- Each file can be up to **50 MB**.
- Word and PowerPoint must be the newer `.docx` / `.pptx` formats. If you have an older `.doc` or `.ppt` file, open it and choose **Save As → .docx** (or **.pptx**) first.
- Scanned PDFs that are just images of text may not read well. A PDF with real, selectable text works best.
  :::

## How to upload

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your assistant">
    From the dashboard, click the assistant you want to add materials to.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add files">
    Go to the materials area and click **Upload**, then choose your files. You
    can select several at once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for processing">
    After uploading, each file is read and indexed in the background. You'll see
    its status change to ready when it's done. Large files take a little longer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

:::tip[You can keep working]
Processing happens in the background, so you can upload a batch and come back later. You don't need to keep the page open.
:::

## What happens behind the scenes

You don't need to understand this to use it, but here's the gist:

<Steps>
  <Step title="It reads the text">
    The assistant pulls the readable text out of your document — including slide
    boundaries and speaker notes for PowerPoint.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It breaks it into pieces">
    Long documents are split into smaller passages so the assistant can pinpoint
    the exact relevant part later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It builds a searchable memory">
    Each passage is indexed so that, when a student asks a question, the
    assistant can instantly find the passages that matter and answer from them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

This is why answers can cite the specific file (and part of the file) they came from.

## Managing your materials

- **Remove a file** any time — the assistant immediately forgets it.
- **Add more** whenever your course evolves. New uploads are available as soon as they finish processing.
- The same file can be shared across multiple assistants if you teach related courses.
