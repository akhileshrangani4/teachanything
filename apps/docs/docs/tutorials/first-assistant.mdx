In this tutorial you'll build a working course assistant from scratch and share it with your students. We'll use an intro biology course as the example, but the steps are the same for any subject.

:::note[Before you start]
You'll need an approved instructor account. If you don't have one yet, see [Getting started](/instructors) for how to sign up and get approved.
:::

## Step 1 — Sign in

Go to [teachanything.ai](https://teachanything.ai) and [log in](https://teachanything.ai/login). You'll land on your [dashboard](https://teachanything.ai/dashboard), which is home base for everything you build.

## Step 2 — Create the assistant

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a new chatbot">
    Click **New chatbot** on your dashboard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    Give it a name students will recognize, like **"BIO 101 Study Helper."**
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write its instructions">
    This is the most important field. Tell it how to behave, in plain English:

    > You are a friendly teaching assistant for an introductory biology course.
    > Answer using only the uploaded course materials. Explain concepts simply,
    > use examples, and encourage students to reason through problems rather
    > than just handing over answers. If something isn't in the materials, say
    > so and suggest the student ask the instructor.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Create**. Your assistant now exists — but it hasn't learned
    anything yet.
  </Step>
</Steps>

For a full breakdown of every setting, see [Create a chatbot](/instructors/create-a-chatbot).

## Step 3 — Add your materials

Now teach it. Open the assistant and upload a few documents — your syllabus, a lecture slide deck, and a reading or two.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload files">
    In the materials area, click **Upload** and select your files. PDFs, Word
    (`.docx`), PowerPoint (`.pptx`), and text/Markdown all work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for processing">
    Each file is read and indexed in the background. Give large files a moment;
    a file is ready once its status says so.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Prefer to point it at a course website instead of uploading? See [Add website content](/instructors/add-website-content).

## Step 4 — Test it

Before students see it, ask it a few questions yourself.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask something from your materials">
    Try a real question, e.g. "What are the stages of mitosis?" The answer
    should reflect your uploaded content.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn on sources and check them">
    Enable **Show sources** in settings so answers cite which file they came
    from, then confirm the citations point to the right material.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Adjust if needed">
    Answers too long, too short, or off-tone? Tweak the [instructions,
    creativity, or answer length](/instructors/customize). Changes take effect
    right away.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 5 — Share it with students

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on sharing">
    In the assistant's settings, make sure sharing is enabled.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the link and send it">
    Share the link on your course page, in an email, or through your LMS.
    Students just click — no account needed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Want it embedded directly in your course site instead of a link? Follow [Add your assistant to your course website](/tutorials/embed-on-your-site).
